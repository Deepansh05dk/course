## The scenario

You'll run an engagement from first meeting to handover, against a customer played by a friend, colleague or mentor. It's the closest thing to the real job you can do on your own, and the strongest single portfolio piece for FDE roles.

## Setting it up

Give your "customer" one of these briefs (they read it; you don't), or have them write their own:

| Brief | The customer | What they say they want | What they don't say up front (reveal only if asked) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A: Freight forwarder** | 200-person company, overloaded customer service | "An AI chatbot for our customers" | Most volume is status questions answered from a slow legacy system; their biggest pain is the 2-day backlog of emails, not chat |
| **B: Insurance claims** | Mid-size insurer, strict compliance | "Automate claims decisions" | Regulators require a human decision; documents are scanned PDFs; their real goal is faster first response |
| **C: IT helpdesk** | 3,000-employee company | "Something like ChatGPT for IT questions" | 30% of tickets are password resets already solvable with self-service nobody finds; security forbids sending tickets to external services without review |

The customer player should also introduce **two surprises** during the engagement (for example: the data is in a different system than they said; a stakeholder who must approve appears late; the budget is half what was assumed).

## The engagement

	caption="The engagement in five sessions with your customer. Each session ends with an artifact they react to."
	code={`flowchart LR

    S2 --> S3["Session 3<br/>POC demo<br/>(with a failure)"]

| Session | You bring | Module |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Discovery** | Questions; afterwards a workflow map with timings, scored use cases, baseline numbers, and a data-access audit | 12 (lessons 2–3) |
| **2. Design** | A one-page design doc with goals, metrics, non-goals, risks; get explicit agreement | 12 (lesson 4) |
| **3. POC demo** | A working system on realistic synthetic data (reuse capstones 1–3), its eval results, and a deliberate failure | 1–11 |
| **4. Pilot results** | Simulated pilot data analysed with confidence intervals, an ROI estimate at observed adoption, and a recommendation | 12 (lesson 5) |
| **5. Handover** | Runbooks, the eval change process, a decision log, and a support plan | 9, 12 |

## What "excellent" looks like

| Area | Excellent |
|---|---|
| **Discovery** | Found the problem behind the request (each brief has one), with numbers |
| **Scope** | A narrow first use case, explicit non-goals, agreed success criteria |
| **Build** | Reuses your capstones sensibly; evals on data shaped like the customer's |
| **Communication** | Every session ends with a clear artifact and next step; surprises handled calmly and transparently |
| **Results** | Honest uncertainty; a recommendation that follows from the evidence |
| **Reflection** | A post-mortem that names your own mistakes and what you'd do differently |

## Deliverables

A write-up of the engagement (discovery notes, design doc, demo recording, pilot analysis, recommendation, handover pack) and a post-mortem. Ask your customer for a short written review of how the engagement felt from their side; it's valuable feedback and a strong addition to a portfolio.
