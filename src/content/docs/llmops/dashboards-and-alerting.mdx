## The signals for an AI feature

Classic services watch the "golden signals": latency, traffic, errors and saturation. AI features need those, plus three more:

| Signal | Metrics | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Latency** | p50 / p95 / p99 total; **time to first token** (TTFT) for streamed features; per-span latency from traces | Users feel TTFT for chat; total time for background jobs |
| **Traffic** | Requests, conversations, model calls per request | A jump in calls per request often means a looping agent |
| **Errors** | Provider errors by type, tool errors, schema validation failures, **fallback rate**, timeouts | Fallback rate is an early warning of provider trouble |
| **Saturation** | Rate-limit headroom, queue depth, concurrency | You hit provider rate limits before you hit CPU limits |
| **Cost** | Per request, per conversation, per tenant, per feature; cache hit rate | Cost per **successful outcome** is the number finance cares about |
| **Quality** | Online eval scores (Module 5), user feedback (Module 2), edit ratio, escalation rate | Slower-moving, but it's what you're actually selling |
| **Safety** | Guardrail blocks, canary hits, policy denials, injection-classifier scores | A spike in policy denials can mean an attack in progress |

Every one of these should be **sliceable** by tenant, feature, AI config version, and model route. Averages hide the customer who's having a terrible week.

## SLOs and error budgets

A **service level objective** turns "Relay should be reliable" into a number you can manage:

- **SLI** (indicator): the share of good events. For example, "chat requests that return a complete, valid answer in under 8 seconds".
- **SLO** (objective): the target. For example, 99% over 28 days.
- **Error budget:** what's left over. 1% of requests may be "bad" this month. If the budget is healthy, ship changes. If it's burning fast, slow down and fix reliability.

Defining "good" for AI is the interesting part. "HTTP 200" isn't enough: a fast answer that fails schema validation, or that fell back to a degraded mode, may count as bad. Agree the definition with product (and, in FDE work, with the customer).

## Alerting on what matters

Good alerts are **urgent, actionable, and about user impact**. Rules of thumb:

1. **Page on symptoms, not causes.** "Error rate above SLO" pages someone. "One provider returned a 529" doesn't; that's what retries and fallbacks are for.
2. **Require duration.** Real incidents last; transient blips don't. A condition that must hold for 10–15 minutes filters most noise.
3. **Use burn-rate alerts for SLOs.** Alert when the error budget is being spent much faster than sustainable, using two windows (for example: burning 14× too fast over both the last 1 hour and the last 5 minutes = page now; a slower burn over 6 hours = a ticket).
4. **Everything else is a ticket or a dashboard**, not a page: cost drift, quality trends, slow decline.
5. **Every page has a runbook link** (lesson 5).

## Quality and cost alerts are different

Quality and cost signals move slowly and need volume to be meaningful, so they rarely deserve a page:

| Signal | Good alerting |
|---|---|
| Online eval score drops | Daily check per segment; a ticket when it drops beyond the noise band you measured in Module 5 |
| User feedback (thumbs down rate) | Weekly trends per feature and tenant; spikes after a deploy compared against the previous config version |
| Cost per conversation | Alert on a sustained rise (for example +30% over a day versus last week), and on **budget burn** per tenant |
| Cost runaway (a looping job) | **This one pages**: spend per hour far above normal. Pair it with automatic limits in the gateway (lesson 3) |
| Canary hits, policy-denial spikes | Page security on canary hits; a ticket for denial trends |

<ThinkFirst question="Define an SLI for Relay's 'draft reply' feature (an internal tool where human agents get an AI draft for each ticket). What counts as a good event? And which one alert would you page on?">

A reasonable SLI: **a draft request is good if** a complete draft is returned within 10 seconds, passes schema and guardrail checks, and was produced by the primary AI config (not a degraded fallback or an error message). Target: 99% over 28 days.

What's deliberately **not** in the SLI: quality scores. They're tracked separately (daily online evals, edit ratio) because they need volume and judgment, not a pager.

The one page: a **multi-window burn-rate alert** on that SLI, for example "burning the monthly error budget more than 14 times faster than sustainable, over both 1 hour and 5 minutes". It catches outages within minutes and ignores blips. Everything else (cost drift, quality trends, single-provider errors) goes to tickets and dashboards.

</ThinkFirst>

- **Stretch:** build Relay's dashboard from your traces and metrics: TTFT, p95, error and fallback rate, cost per conversation, online eval score, each sliceable by tenant and config version.
