<Incident title="The confident customs answer" severity="SEV2">
An agent asked Relay whether lithium batteries could ship to Norway by sea. Relay answered "Yes, with standard packaging," in a calm, authoritative paragraph with no sources. The agent passed it on. The shipment was stopped at the port, because the policy requires a hazmat label. The model's answer was **wrong 5% of the time** on this kind of question, but the UI made it look **right 100% of the time**.
</Incident>

Your system **will** be wrong sometimes. Evals reduce how often; UX decides **how much damage** each wrong answer does. The goal isn't to make users trust the AI. It's **calibrated trust**: trust it where it's reliable, check it where it isn't, and make checking cheap.

## Five patterns that limit the damage

| Pattern | What it does | Relay example |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Show sources** | Every claim links to its evidence | "Batteries require a hazmat label [Policy §4.2]" with a click to open the excerpt |
| **2. Say "I don't know"** | Honest refusal when the evidence is missing | "The policy library doesn't cover shipping to Norway by sea. Escalate to the compliance team." |
| **3. Show the work** | Tool calls, searches and steps are visible | "Searched 'battery shipping rules' → 3 policy excerpts → checked shipment record" |
| **4. Signal uncertainty honestly** | Cues based on **real signals**, not vibes | "Low confidence: sources disagree" when retrieval returns conflicting policies |
| **5. Make correction effortless** | Edit, regenerate, report, undo, all one click | An inline "This is wrong" that captures the correct answer |

## 1. Citations: the most powerful trust tool

Citations change the user's job from "trust or don't" to "verify in two seconds". Good citation UX:

- **Inline and specific:** the marker sits next to the claim it supports, not in a list at the end.
- **One click to the evidence:** show the exact excerpt, highlighted, not just the document title.
- **Verifiable by code, not just displayed:** check that every cited source was actually retrieved for this answer, and that quoted text exists in it (Module 4). A citation to a document the model never saw is worse than no citation.

In the AI SDK protocol, sources are first-class parts (`source-url`, `source-document`), so a Python backend can emit them and the React UI can render them as chips.

## 2. "I don't know" is a feature

The model will try to be helpful, so refusing must be **designed in**:

- In the prompt: "If the provided policy excerpts don't answer the question, say so and suggest escalation. Don't use general knowledge for policy questions."
- In the pipeline: if retrieval returns nothing relevant (low scores), don't even ask the model to answer. Return the "not covered" response directly.
- In the UI: make "not covered" a **useful** state, with a one-click "Escalate to compliance" button and the question pre-filled.
- In evals: include unanswerable questions and score refusals as **correct** (Module 5).

## 3–4. Show the work, signal uncertainty from real signals

Never ask the model "how confident are you (0–100)?" and display the number. Self-reported confidence is poorly calibrated. Use **signals you can measure**:

| Signal | Shows |
|---|---|
| Retrieval scores below a threshold, or no results | "Limited sources found" |
| Retrieved sources conflict (different versions, dates) | "Sources disagree: check §4.2 (2025) vs §4.2 (2023)" |
| Validation failed and the repair loop was used | Route to review, or show a soft warning |
| The question category has a low eval score | "Answers about customs are often incomplete. Verify before sending." |
| A tool errored and the answer is partial | "Shipment system unavailable: status may be outdated" |

<ThinkFirst question="Relay will answer customs questions, where a wrong answer can mean a seized shipment or a fine. Design the answer UI: what does the agent see, and what must they do before the answer goes to a customer?">

A strong design adds friction **in proportion to the stakes**:

1. **Answer with inline, verified citations** to specific policy sections, opening a highlighted excerpt on click.
2. **A visible category banner:** "Customs: verify before sending", based on the category's measured error rate, not the model's self-confidence.
3. **Explicit uncertainty states:** sources missing, so a "not covered" state with a pre-filled escalation to the compliance team. Sources conflicting, so both are shown with dates and the answer is withheld.
4. **A confirmation step before sending to a customer:** the agent ticks "I checked the cited policy" (logged), or the draft routes to a compliance reviewer for specific high-risk sub-topics (dangerous goods).
5. **One-click "This is wrong"** that captures the correct answer and source, feeding the eval set.

Low-stakes categories (tracking status) get none of this friction. **Calibrated friction is the design goal.**

</ThinkFirst>

<Practice>

- **Warm-up:** collect five AI answers from any assistant you use. For each, write down what in the UI made you trust or distrust it.
- **Stretch:** in your Relay UI, render citation chips from a list of source IDs, with a side panel that shows the excerpt and highlights the cited sentence.
- **Boss level:** implement **citation verification**: given an answer with `[S1]`-style markers and the retrieved chunks, check that every marker refers to a retrieved chunk and that each cited sentence overlaps meaningfully with its source (start with simple word overlap). Show unverified claims with a warning style, and report the citation precision on 20 test answers.

</Practice>
