Three job titles dominate AI-engineering hiring in 2026. They share most of their skills and differ in **where you spend your time** and **who you answer to**.

## Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)

You **sit with the customer** and make the AI product work in *their* world, with their data, systems, security rules and people.

- **A typical week:** a discovery call with operations staff, reading a CSV export that turns out to have 14 date formats, writing a connector to a 15-year-old SOAP API, demoing a pilot to a VP, and debugging why the deployment can't reach the model endpoint from inside their VPC.
- **What's distinctive:** you own the outcome from discovery through system design, implementation, evaluation and production rollout. You're judged on whether the customer gets value, not on how elegant the code is.
- **Who hires:** model labs, AI-native startups, data/AI platforms (Palantir popularized the title), consultancies, defense tech.
- **Demand:** one analysis counted FDE and adjacent postings rising from about 640 (April 2025) to about 5,300 (April 2026). Treat the exact numbers loosely; the trend is clear.

## Applied AI Engineer

You **build AI features into a product** and you're accountable for their quality in production.

- **A typical week:** reading 100 production traces to categorize failures, adding a reranker, writing an LLM-judge for "did the answer cite the right policy," cutting p95 latency with streaming and caching, and evaluating whether a new model can replace the current one.
- **What's distinctive:** depth on reliability. A common 2026 framing is that the job is "reliability engineering around models, not model building."

## GenAI Engineer

This is the title enterprises and consultancies use most. Postings lean toward **building blocks and platforms**.

- **A typical week:** building an ingestion pipeline for 50,000 documents, wiring an agent with LangGraph, exposing an internal system as an MCP server, and deploying on Azure AI Foundry or Bedrock.
- **What's distinctive:** more tool- and platform-specific requirements, and occasionally fine-tuning (LoRA/QLoRA).

## Side by side

| | FDE | Applied AI Eng. | GenAI Eng. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main stakeholder | The customer | Product team and users | Business units and clients |
| Core output | A deployment that proves value | A reliable AI feature | AI pipelines and platforms |
| Weighted skills | Integration, field debugging, scoping, communication | Evals, context engineering, observability, cost | RAG, agents, MCP, cloud AI platforms |
| Customer-facing | High | Low | Medium |
| Typical experience asked | ~3–6 yrs, but strong portfolios get in earlier | 2–5 yrs | 3–5 yrs, with 1–2 in GenAI |

## Which one should you target first?

<ThinkFirst
	title="Career drill"
	question="You have about 2 years of experience, strong frontend skills, and you're comfortable with Python/FastAPI. Which role would you target first, and what is the biggest gap you'd need to close?"
>

**Recommended path: Applied AI Engineer first, then FDE.**

- Your frontend skill is a real differentiator. Many AI engineers can build the pipeline, but few can ship a UX that handles streaming, citations, approvals and wrong answers well.
- FastAPI already covers the backend half of most AI products.
- The gap is **LLM-specific engineering**: evals, retrieval, agents, observability, cost. Modules 1–11 cover it.
- FDE comes next. FDE interviews test end-to-end ownership and customer judgment, which Module 12 and Capstone 4 build. FDE experience bars are often higher, so **deployed projects with honest post-mortems** matter more than certificates.

</ThinkFirst>

:::note[What none of these roles usually require]
Training models from scratch, heavy maths, or research publications. Fine-tuning appears in some postings, but the consistent industry advice is that prompting plus retrieval solves most production problems more cheaply. We treat fine-tuning as a "know when to use it" skill in Module 11.
:::

- **Boss level:** create a `career.md` in your repo with a skills matrix (skill × "can explain / can build / have shipped"). Update it at the end of every module, and you'll watch the "shipped" column fill up.

</Practice>
